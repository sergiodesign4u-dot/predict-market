/* THIS BAR HAD NO STATE ON EITHER AXIS, and it is the one control in the product that is only
   ever touched: it is display:none from 640px up. So the press is the state that matters here,
   and the hover is the one nobody will see; a tap used to get no reply at all, on all 104
   screens, on the only navigation a phone has.
   THE GROUND ANSWERS AND THE COLOUR DOES NOT. In this bar colour is the current-tab signal and
   nothing else, muted against brass. If pointing at a tab pulled its label toward the brass,
   the one thing the bar says at a glance would be said by two different things, and on a phone
   a hover can stick after a tap, so the wrong tab would keep claiming to be current. The two
   roles are the ones the chip family already uses: --tint-hover, a translucent wash that
   lightens on graphite and darkens on chalk by itself, and --bg-pressed, the one press ground
   in the system. Measured on this bar they are rgba(255,255,255,.06) and #191b1f over #1c1f24
   in the dark, #211f19 at 10 per cent and #f3f1eb over #f8f6f0 in the light, so on chalk both
   darken and the wash is the bigger step of the two. That inverts nothing under a finger: a
   tap fires both states at once and the press, later here at equal specificity, is the one
   that shows. It would only read backwards as a hover LEFT behind after the finger lifts,
   which is the platform's sticky-hover behaviour and is every hover's problem, not this one's.
   Both rules name the <li> to reach (0,2,2), which is what the current-page rule above costs.
   Written at the button's own (0,2,1) they would die on the tab you are standing on, and that
   is the tab a person taps most. Source order decides the tie, so they stay here, below it. */
.bottom-nav li button:hover{background:var(--tint-hover)}
.bottom-nav li button:active{background:var(--bg-pressed)}
/* No :focus-visible here, and it was measured rather than assumed, because a fixed bar is where
   a ring gets cut. Chromium at 360x720, tab bar stuck: the base.css ring computes to 2px solid
   --focus-ring at 2px offset, no ancestor of the button clips anything (overflow is visible the
   whole way up to <body>), and the nav's own z-index paints the ring over the feed rather than
   under it, so the top stroke shows 3px above the bar. Only the bottom stroke falls past the
   viewport edge, 4px of it, and a ring with three sides on screen is a ring you can see. An
   inset offset would buy that fourth stroke by moving the other three onto the control. */
.bn-bal{background:var(--bg-control);border:1px solid var(--border-hairline);color:var(--text-primary);border-radius:var(--radius-10)}
.bottom-nav{border-top:1px solid var(--edge-groove-dark);box-shadow:0 1px 0 var(--edge-groove-light)}
