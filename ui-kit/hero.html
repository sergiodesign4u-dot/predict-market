One placement, and it earns the page twice over

A component with one placement cannot be checked against itself, and this one is 166 lines: a featured card with a photograph and a graph, a ranked list, a brand tile and a trust strip, all in a grid that goes from 351x1267 on a phone to 969x505 on a desktop. Everything about it is a decision taken once, with nothing to compare it against.

The level and its reason stay on the organisms page. What this page holds is the two things the single placement hid: a hover that existed in one theme only, and a hover that was worse than nothing in the other one.

The worst number this pass found, and it was already shipped

A link in this block took its hover colour from the LIT stop of an action gradient, a pale brass meant to sit UNDER dark ink. The theme does not move it, because a mid-luminance metal reads on both stones. As a hover COLOUR it is fine on graphite, 9.57:1, and on chalk it is 1.71:1.

In daylight the link VANISHED at the moment a person pointed at it, from 6.96:1 at rest. A role that means "the lit stop of a gradient" is not a role that means "ink", and the two are indistinguishable in the theme somebody happens to be working in.

The second, in the same block: a headline hover read the strong ink, which is white on graphite and INK ON CHALK, and the rest colour there is also ink on chalk. So in the Vault the headline lit up, and in daylight the hover painted 14.85:1 over 14.85:1: nothing, in the theme nobody was checking.

A STATE THAT ONLY EXISTS IN ONE THEME DOES NOT EXIST. Measured, not guessed, and the answer was not a brighter ink, because in daylight there is none left.

The tile is a claim, not a control: there is no link and no button inside it, so no ground may change under a finger, and the only thing a press can honestly say is that the lift above it is not stuck. It moves the tile back to where it already rests rather than anywhere new, which is why it is the one transform in the whole press pass.

The featured pair, and the sentence that got it wrong

The YES and NO on the featured market are yes / no's now, and until 2026-08-06 this file said the opposite. What was written here was TRUE and was the wrong conclusion: every selector in the pair's file hangs off its own class, so not one of them reaches this button.

The answer to a control being out of a family's reach is to put it in reach, not to draw it again. Measured before it was moved, the two are the same control to the value: the same tinted fill, the same edge, the same ink.

The same file gives the hot list's rows the ink press for the same reason the card's question takes it: the row is a grid and only the name is the link, so a stone would paint one cell of five and read as a highlight on the title rather than a press on the row. 6.40:1 in the Vault, 7.22:1 in daylight, measured on the card it stands on.

The rule

Every state is checked in both themes. One placement means one person looked at it, in one theme, once.

A role is used for what its name says. A lit gradient stop is not an ink, and the difference is invisible on graphite.

Out of reach is a reason to move a control, never a reason to redraw it.

The anti-rule

Never take a hover colour without measuring it in daylight. 1.71:1, shipped, on the most prominent link on the home screen.

Never write a state whose rest and hover are the same colour in one theme. 14.85:1 over 14.85:1 is a hover that exists in the stylesheet and nowhere else.

Never let a claim behave like a control. The brand tile has no link inside it, so nothing may change under a finger except the lift coming back down.
