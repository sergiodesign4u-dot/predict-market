The height of the row is the same at both widths, and only the question stretches. It clamps to two lines, so a long market cannot push the price off the row or make one row twice its neighbour.

The photograph is written on the element, and it is the one thing the root rule names by name: a datum, an event photograph, and a value a script writes at run time are not styling. This is the second of the three.

The press goes on the link, not on the row around it

The whole row IS the link, so the ground goes on the <a>. The <li> around it owns only the groove between rows, and a state drawn there would sit OUTSIDE the thing being pushed: a 1px line changing colour while the row it separates does nothing.

This is the tap target of nine event screens, on mobile, where the hover never fires at all. The row is flat at rest, so only the ground moves, and the question keeps the brass the hover gives it: one axis, and the axis that a finger can see.

See more is the same chip as Load more elsewhere, and it answered the pointer on the way in while saying nothing when pushed. Same fix, written after the hover at equal specificity so source order decides. Its inset top bevel is the chip's own edge rather than a lift, so it stays; there is no outer shadow to drop.

31 rows, 27 thumbnails, and 0 rows misaligned

Four rows in the product have no photograph, and this page said they were 58px out of line with the rows above them. Measured in a browser on 2026-08-08, they are not. All four are on terms.html, they are the whole of that list, and not one of them is a market: the block is headed "The other documents" and the rows read Privacy Policy / not built. A list where no row has a thumbnail is internally consistent, so every question in it starts at the same 68 at 390 and the same 586.5 at 1280.

A count without a grouping is a defect looking for a screen. "4 of 31 rows have no thumbnail" was read from the source and was true. What it left out is that the 4 are four of four on one screen and the 27 are 27 of 27 on nine others, which is the difference between a broken layout and two lists that each know what they are.

The arrangement that DOES break is a mixed list, and it was built to check. Cloning one row into the terms list and giving it a thumbnail puts that row's question at 126 against the other four at 68, and at 644.5 against 586.5: 58px, at both widths, exactly the number the row named. That arrangement exists nowhere in the product.

So the contract is written rather than a rule added. The thumbnail is optional and the LIST decides: every row carries one or no row does. A rule for a mixed list would be a rule with no reader, and the day a market genuinely has no photograph the placeholder is a thing to design rather than a thing to guess now. docs/backlog.md 60, closed.

The rule

A state is drawn on the thing that is pressed. If the anchor is the row, the row's ground is the anchor's ground, and the list item is furniture.

A question clamps. Two lines, always, so a list of markets is a list and not a column of paragraphs.

The anti-rule

Never put the press on the list item. The ground would land outside the link, which is the part a finger is actually on.

Never mix a row with a thumbnail and a row without one in the same list. Measured at 58px of misalignment at both widths. The column comes from the child, so a row with no child has no column, and the reader sees two components rather than one list.

Never let this plate become a second feed. It is a way out of a dead end, three or four rows deep; a list that scrolls is the feed's job and the feed already exists.
