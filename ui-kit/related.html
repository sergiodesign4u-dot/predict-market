The press goes on the link, not on the row around it

The whole row IS the link, so the ground goes on the <a>. The <li> around it owns only the groove between rows, and a state drawn there would sit OUTSIDE the thing being pushed: a 1px line changing colour while the row it separates does nothing.

A count without a grouping is a defect looking for a screen. "4 of 31 rows have no thumbnail" was read from the source and was true, and "20 of 50" would be read the same way and be true again. What both leave out is that the rows without a picture are whole lists on document pages and the rows with one are whole lists on event screens, which is the difference between a broken layout and two kinds of list that each know what they are.

The arrangement that DOES break is a mixed list, and it was built to check. Cloning one row into the terms list and giving it a thumbnail puts that row's question at 126 against the other four at 68, and at 644.5 against 586.5: 58px, at both widths, exactly the number the row named. That arrangement exists nowhere in the product.

So the contract is written rather than a rule added. The thumbnail is optional and the LIST decides: every row carries one or no row does. A rule for a mixed list would be a rule with no reader, and the day a market genuinely has no photograph the placeholder is a thing to design rather than a thing to guess now. docs/backlog.md 60, closed.

The rule

A state is drawn on the thing that is pressed. If the anchor is the row, the row's ground is the anchor's ground, and the list item is furniture.

A question clamps. Two lines, always, so a list of markets is a list and not a column of paragraphs.

The anti-rule

Never put the press on the list item. The ground would land outside the link, which is the part a finger is actually on.

Never mix a row with a thumbnail and a row without one in the same list. Measured at 58px of misalignment at both widths. The column comes from the child, so a row with no child has no column, and the reader sees two components rather than one list.

Never let this plate become a second feed. It is a way out of a dead end, three or four rows deep; a list that scrolls is the feed's job and the feed already exists.
