Separators (the engraved groove)

One separator, used everywhere: a dark hairline with a faint light highlight 1px below it, so it reads as a groove carved into the surface, never a flat black line. The reusable class is .groove-sep (recipe: border-top:1px solid var(--edge-groove-dark); box-shadow:inset 0 1px 0 var(--edge-groove-light)). It carries the facts strip, section tops, footer and the bet breakdown rows.

Spacing (the 2px grid)

Every gap, padding and margin is a multiple of 2. Primary steps 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 20 / 24 (the 8pt backbone); 2 / 6 / 10 / 14 fine-tune small elements. The one rule: the value is divisible by 2 - 16, never 15. Within a group gaps stay small (4-10); between groups they open up (16-24) - small-inside, bigger-outside.

Corner radius

Softly rounded, never sharp, and every radius is even - the 2px rule applies here too (no 9, no 7). 10 is the default (buttons, inputs, confirm, cards); chips and dropdowns at 12, dialogs at 16, the bottom sheet's top edge at 20, and only real pills (the sort filter, feed YES / NO) go fully round.

How betting works here

Predict Market turns the events you follow into a real stake. Pick an event, read the odds in plain language, and back YES or NO. You see the current odds, a one-line why, and how the event resolves before you put in a cent. The minimum bet is one dollar, and you can browse and build your bet before you connect a wallet.

Why the odds move

The odds are a live price set by what people bet, not a fixed quote. When more money backs YES, YES costs more and NO costs less. Your payout depends on the odds at the moment you bet, not only on the outcome, so reading an event early and being right is worth more.

Common questions

Do I need crypto to start?
No. You can browse events and build a bet with no wallet. You add funds by card or crypto only when you confirm.
What is the smallest bet?
One dollar. The default is five.
How does an event resolve?
Each event states its resolution rule up front. The team resolves it against the real-world outcome, and you can see the record of resolved events.

Feedback and overlays

Transient and outcome UI. Toasts confirm quietly - success takes a brass tick, and the error toast carries no red, since green and red are the YES / NO outcome only, so its salience rides icon weight and copy. Win and loss are invoked overlays: the win marks the result without lighting a fuse, the loss explains what happened and its next step is not "bet again".

The header is lean on desktop and compact on mobile; logged out it swaps the balance and avatar for Sign in / Sign up.
