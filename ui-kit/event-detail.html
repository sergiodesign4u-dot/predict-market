Specificity is per PROPERTY, and this file paid for it twice

A section carries a label, and the column heads nested inside its blocks are not that label. The first attempt made the label rule a CHILD selector, which is wrong twice over: the label is a grandchild, so it lost its own rule, and a section whose label happened to be a direct child would still have caught a nested head. The distinction is not depth, it is which block the heading belongs to.

Then the fix was written and it was still short. The rule that promotes a nested head out-specifies the section label, but only for what it DECLARES: the label's uppercase, its tracking and its brass went on applying to a heading that had just been promoted into their reach. A heading that belongs to a nested block has to say so for every property the outer rule sets.

The same shape appears once more in the bets table, for the same reason and in the same afternoon.

What left, and what a region may still say

The layout left: the two columns are a pattern, and the plate the main column stands on is in the page frame. Three controls left on 2026-08-06, the bookmark, the share and the alert over the head, and they are the icon button's tile face.

What this file may still say about them is exactly one thing: WHERE the row sits over the head. The ground, the corner, the ink, the mark, the hover, the press and the favourited state are all one file away.

A market that has settled replaces the bet panel with a resolved panel, and there is one placement of it. Inside it the state block takes its third skin: no ground, no edge, and the actions become a full-width column, because that panel is the width of a sidebar and two buttons side by side in 280px are two buttons nobody can read.

The rule

A screen file owns its own parts and nothing else. The shell is a pattern, the plate is the page, the controls are atoms, and what is left is a head, a facts row and a resolution block.

A heading belongs to a block, not to a depth. Selectors that reason about how deep something is will be wrong the first time the markup gains a wrapper.

The anti-rule

Never out-specify a rule for one property and assume the rest are handled. Uppercase, tracking and brass all went on applying to a heading that had been promoted out of their reach on the property that mattered.

Never use depth to say what a thing belongs to. The label is a grandchild and the nested head is a child, and the child selector got both of them backwards.

Never let a screen file draw a control. Three of them stood here, and the only correct sentence left is where the row sits.
