How this is read

The component in the markup it ships with, quoted from the frozen kit, inside the product's own wrapper and painted by components/index.css alone. Each frame is a page of its own, so the width under the title is a real viewport and the media queries answer to it. One row per CONTROL, in one column, and a scope is present only where the scope is what the row is about: twelve of the sixteen combinations the product wears were measured against the same markup with no scope above them and came out identical over thirteen properties, so a plate around those would be showing the reader a dialog to prove something about a button. The numbers for every row are in the axis table further down, written once.

What it is

A label a person picks between, when the choice is small, immediate and does not leave the screen. Four time ranges under a chart, three sort orders over a comment thread, eight categories in a strip, twelve sub-categories in a rail, five amounts on a sheet. It carries a value rather than doing a thing, so what it looks like afterwards matters more than what it looks like while it is pressed.

Anatomy

Which part is which, in the product's own words. Every class named here is one this file styles, and the build fails if it is not.

  • .chip - the atom, and it is four declarations: a pointer, the body face, no underline and a centred label. The last two arrived on 2026-08-07 with the ELEMENT: a chip that navigates is an <a> now rather than a <button> wrapped in one, and both values were things the <button> tag had been supplying for free - one of them written down twice in two other files, about the wrapper. Everything a person can see belongs to one of the five faces below, and a base class is the atom or it is a face, never both - the day this file's one face was written on .chip itself, 722 elements took values nobody had chosen for them.
  • .chip.chip-rail - a segmented control on a plate: no edge, no ground, --text-muted, a 10 corner, 12px semibold.
  • .chip.chip-quiet - a scrolling strip: transparent on a transparent 1px band, a pill, 13px. The band is not decoration - it means the box is the SIZE's answer, so a chip does not grow by 2px at the moment it is chosen and given a brass edge.
  • .chip.chip-nav - the category band: --bg-chip on a --bevel-notice edge at 14px, the loudest chip in the product, because on a phone it is the one control that changes what the page is about.
  • .chip.chip-lane - the sub-category rail, and the only face in this system that answers TWO surfaces: a hairline pill in a scroller at 380, a full-width row with the count pushed to the far side above 900. The face carries that media query, because what changes with the window is the surface and not the placement.
  • .chip.chip-amount - a form control in a sheet: --bg-control on a hairline, a 10 corner. What a chip looks like when it is one of the things you fill in.
  • .chip-ic, .chip-cnt - the two parts. A category's mark, drawn in currentColor so the chip's ink is its ink, and a sub-category's count, mono because it is a figure. Both are here rather than with the rail for a reason gate 24 supplied: they change with the CONTROL's state, which is what a part of a face does, and leaving them behind made the atom read as containing a component.
  • .chip.sel and li[aria-current="page"] .chip - the chosen one, and they are one rule. A brass TINT, brass ink, bold, a halo. .sel where a class survives the pointer leaving, aria-current where the chip points at the page you are on, which is the correct thing for navigation and is not going to be replaced by a class.

States, as they measure

One block per distinct face. Rest says what the face IS; every row under it says only what that state MOVES.

How this is read

The frames above are the rendering; this is the reading of them. Every number was taken in a browser with a REAL pointer, a real Tab and the mouse button really held down (ui-kit/_verify/states.cjs), because a hover value is the cascade resolved and cannot be read out of a declaration. Nothing here is a state faked with a stand class. Two elements that measure the same in all four states in both themes are one answer, however many screens carry them, and that grouping is computed rather than chosen. The ring is the tenth value and it joined on 2026-08-05: outline is drawn outside the border box, so it is not the face in the strict sense, and for a stage this page said so and reported that focus moves nothing on 93 groups, which is the one state whose whole content it was not reading. The numbers go stale the moment a rule changes, so each block records the files it was measured from and python3 ui-kit/_states.py fails when their bytes move.

When to use

The judgement a generator cannot read out of css, and the reason ui-kit/authored/chip.md exists.

For a set of two to five values a person switches between on the spot, inside a container that gives them their ground: a trough, a rail, a segment. For a set that has to be scrolled sideways, the strip faces exist and say so.

Not for a set that has to be scrolled and read at once. Five is a rail, twelve is a menu that has forgotten it has one.

The rule, and the anti-rule

One sentence to follow and one mistake worth naming. An anti-rule has to name the component that should have been used instead, or it is a complaint rather than an address, and the build checks that it does.

Do

A selected chip is a tint, never a fill. A solid brass ground is the primary action's own face, and a rail of chips is not asking anybody to commit to anything. DESIGN.md decides it twice, under Chips and under the One-Accent Rule, and components/quick.css records what it cost the one time a chip took the fill: a lit brass $20 sitting directly above a lit brass Add funds.

One recipe for chosen, and it is one rule for the whole family. --tint-brass-09 inside a --tint-brass-45 edge with --text-brass-chip, bold, and a halo that is a glow with negative spread rather than a lift. The edge is written where the face HAS one: the recipe colours what a chip already has and adds no parts, so the rail chip is borderless at rest and stays borderless and no box moves.

The container gives the ground, not the chip. Every rule in components/chip.css is scoped to .app-case, and the three quiet faces paint no ground at rest. That is what lets one control stand in a --bg-well trough, on a stone band and on a bare section without any of them looking wrong.

A face is an answer to the surface the control stands on. Five faces, five surfaces, and the fifth answers two of them because its surface changes with the window. That sentence is the whole map: it is why the amount is not the rail chip with a ground, and why reading the lane at one width put it in the wrong atom for a day.

Never

Never draw it again in the file of whatever holds it. That is exactly how it came to exist four times: comments.css owned the sort segment because the segment stands in the comment controls, tabs.css owned the chart range, quick.css owned the amounts, catnav.css owned three more, and every one of those files was right about its container and wrong about the control.

Never give the atom a face. .chip carries what every chip agrees on and nothing else. A sixth surface takes a sixth face; a difference of four pixels takes none, which is what the Trending sub-filter turned out to be against 525 identical chips in the header.

Never let a part stay behind. If a chip grows something that lives inside it and changes when the chip does, it comes here and takes a chip name. Gate 24 will say so anyway, and it is faster to hear it from this paragraph.

Seen: ui-kit/docs/backlog.md S35, S38, S41 and S46, and ui-kit/docs/defects.md, which records the measurement that ended the duplicate.
