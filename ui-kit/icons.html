Icons

A set is a grid with rules, not a sheet of pictures. Below: the anatomy of one cell, optical balance, and an audit of all 52 glyphs against the paint, which is where the field turns out never to have been set and where the centre has drifted. This axis had never been measured: the census counted controls, and an icon is not a control.

Anatomy

  1. 1Cell 24 x 24 - viewBox="0 0 24 24" on the whole set. Not "about 24": one grid for every glyph is the only thing that lets a new one be drawn without being held up against its neighbours. This part the set already has, on 5,180 readings, and it is the reason the rest is fixable.
  2. 2Safe field 2 - paint lives in 2..22. Those two modules are not emptiness, they are headroom: the glyph stands beside text, inside a button and inside a circle, and with no headroom it touches the edge of the circle.
  3. 3The stroke paints outward from the contour - a 1.6 stroke puts 0.8 of paint past the geometry on every side. The boundary is checked against the PAINT, not against the curve, or the safe field is eaten by half a stroke and the audit reads a module too kind.
  4. 4Optical centre, not geometric. A glyph with a heavy bottom, a basket or a bell, is centred by eye: evenness is counted by sight and not by coordinates. The brass cross above is the geometric centre, so a deliberate optical shift can be seen rather than argued about.
  5. 5Ends on the grid. A stroke end lands on a whole module, or at 16px it smears across half a pixel and the glyph reads as dirty.

Optical balance

The same bounding box and the same weight are different things. A circle inscribed in the field a square fills looks smaller: the eye counts area, not the rectangle around it. So a set is deliberately not one size, and the four below are the argument, each measured against its paint.

The chevron is the case. It is the second most used glyph in the product and it paints a third of the cell's height. Beside a full-height glyph it does not read as a smaller icon, it reads as a mistake. That is not a drawing to enlarge, it is a decision the set has never taken: either the chevron is drawn to the field like the rest, or the set declares a class of small marks and says which glyphs are in it.

There are two families, and four jobs are drawn in both

Of 44 icons on one screen, 14 are filled and 30 are stroked. The filled half is Solar Bold, referenced through <use> from a sprite, drawn to a 2 module field, no stroke at all. The stroked half is hand-drawn inline at 1.6. They are not variants of one set; they are two sets, and they stand next to each other in the same header band.

Four jobs exist in both. Not two weights of one mark, which would be a system: two different drawings, from two different hands, of the same idea.

This is a decision, not a defect to sweep. Two families can be right: filled for a state that is on, stroked for one that is off, is a rule many sets keep. But nobody wrote that rule here, and the evidence is that it is not being kept: the bookmark is filled in the card and stroked in the footer, for the same meaning. Either the rule is written and the four pairs are made to obey it, or one family wins.

The stroke does not hold, and it is off by three times

.ic declares stroke-width:1.6 once, in user units, inside a 24 unit box. User units scale with the box. So the line is not 1.6 anywhere: it is 1.6 multiplied by however large the icon was asked to be, and the set renders at six sizes. The same glyph, at every size the product actually uses:

Measured on the real screens, in CSS pixels: 0.90px at 12, 1.07 at 16, 1.20 at 18, 1.33 at 20, 1.47 at 22, 2.67 at 40. That is a factor of three across one set, and it runs the wrong way at both ends. At 0.90px the line is under one device pixel at DPR 1 and the glyph smears; at 2.67px the icon is a heavier weight than the type beside it, so the trust row reads as bolder than its own heading. The set has no optical weight. It has one geometric weight that slides.

.ic-sm makes it worse rather than better: it is the smallest box at 12px and it declares the thicker user-unit stroke, 1.8 against 1.6, which after the scale still lands at 0.90px, the thinnest line in the product.

The safe field, measured against the paint

The smallest distance from painted edge to cell edge, for all 52. The stroked numbers all end in .2 because they are a whole-module contour plus the 0.8 of stroke that paints outside it, which is the arithmetic of rule 3 showing up in the data.

A method note, because the first pass of this got it wrong. A sweep run over the live feed reported thirteen glyphs inside the field. It was reading the stroke off getComputedStyle of the child, and a <use> inside .ic inherits stroke-width:1.6 whether or not it paints a stroke, so every filled glyph was credited with 0.8 of paint it does not have and lost a module of field. An inherited property is not a painted one, and the fix is to ask whether the element actually strokes before spending half of it.

Stroked, 33 glyphs: field 2.2 on 15, 3.2 on 10, 4.2 on 6, 5.2 on 2. Filled, 15 glyphs: 2.0 on 13, 1.0 on one, 3.0 on one. Not ours, 4: 0.0, 0.0, 0.7, 1.2, correctly outside the rule. So exactly one glyph in the owned set is inside the field, i-cat-politics at 1.0, and the real finding is the other way round: the bought family holds 2.0 on 13 of 15, and the hand-drawn family has never had a field at all. It has four.

Two modules is the target and it is not a preference. An icon sits in a 36px circular button at 22px: 2 modules of headroom is 1.8px of air between the paint and the button's own border, and that is already tight. Below 2 the glyph touches. Above 4 it stops reading as the same size as its neighbours, which is the chevron's problem in section 02.

The centre has drifted on twelve glyphs

Measured against the geometric centre, in modules. Some of it is right: a shield at -0.5 and a bell at 0.0 are optically centred, which is rule 4 being obeyed. Some of it is not. sort at -2.0 across is a glyph drawn from x=4 with ragged right ends, so it is not optically centred, it is left-aligned inside a centred box. trend-up at -2.0 down and music at -1.7 / -0.8 are the same thing on the other axes. Those three are drawings to correct; the rest are within half a module and are left alone, because a half module at 16px is a third of a pixel and moving it buys nothing.

The rules, and what this decides

Rule. One cell, one field, one weight. viewBox="0 0 24 24" on every glyph; paint in 2..22, checked against the paint; the stroke reads a token and lands on a whole module. Anti-rule. An icon is never the only name of an action, and a glyph is never redrawn because the old one looked slightly wrong in one place. That is how a set gets seven jobs and eight drawings.

What goes into the consolidation

  1. 1The stroke becomes a size-aware token, not a constant. One declaration of 1.6 in user units is what makes the line run 0.90 to 2.67. The fix is a stroke per rendered size, so 12, 16, 18, 20, 22 and 40 all paint the same weight on screen.
  2. 2The safe field is declared at 2 and the stroked family is redrawn to it. Nothing there is inside the field; the four fields it does have run 2.2 to 5.2, and the chevron sits 5.2 out. The filled family already holds 2.0 on 13 of 15, which is what a set drawn to a rule looks like and is the target.
  3. 3The two families are declared or one of them goes. Four jobs are drawn in both and the bookmark uses both for one meaning. This is the only item here that is a product decision rather than a value.
  4. 4The three duplicate pairs inside the stroked family are collapsed, each to the drawing with the count.
  5. 5The provider marks get a written exception and stop being four untokenised hex values with no rule attached.
  6. 6.ic-sm is deleted as a size. It is the smallest box carrying the thickest user-unit stroke, which is the exact inversion of what a small glyph needs, and it exists only because nobody had measured the rendered result.
