Action bar

One or two actions held at the foot of a column, sticky by default and flat where the page has no scroll to hold them against. It is the arrangement and nothing else: how the actions share the row, where the row sits, and what happens when there is only one of them.

Live

Cut out of wallet.html byte for byte, with the ancestor chain it has there, and painted by components/index.css alone. Every string is the shipped microcopy, because none of it was retyped. The wallet, which carries the .static variant. The other two screens carry .flat, so between them the three screens show two of the three positions this bar has.

This is the only place the pattern exists outside the product. A component has a specimen as well as a stand; a pattern has neither, because step 3 took patterns out of gate 24's corpus deliberately: that gate compares what a component contains against what it contains on the screens, and a pattern contains whatever the screen puts in it. So this frame is not an illustration of the pattern, it is the test of it.

Variants

.cta-bar.static

Position only, for a page whose column does not scroll under it.

.cta-bar.flat

Position and surface: the stone, the hairline and the two rounded corners come off, and it reads as a row at the end of the content. The three declarations that take the surface away are in components/account.css and not here, which is the one declared paint exception of the patterns step.

What it composes

The COMPONENTS in it, named by a person. The Anatomy above answers which class is which; this answers what each part contributes, which is the question a class list cannot reach.

  • account - the bar's surface: the stone, the hairline along the top edge and the two rounded corners, plus the .flat variant that takes all three away. It is what the bar is MADE of; this file is where it stands.
  • button - the actions. Each is an <a> at flex:1 holding a button at width:100%, so two actions split the row evenly and one fills it without either button learning a width of its own.

When to use it

When a screen has one action that is the reason a person came to it, and the content above is long enough that the action would otherwise scroll away. The wallet, the profile and How It Works are the three, and each has exactly one such action.

Never twice on a screen. A sticky bar is the loudest thing on a phone, and a screen with two has told a person nothing about which one to press.

By hand: when the action is not the reason the screen exists. Two actions in a row at the foot of a card, a pair of links under a form, an inline confirm inside a panel - all of those are a flex row and two buttons, written where they stand. Reaching for this pattern makes them sticky, which is a claim about their importance that the screen has not earned. The test is whether the action should follow a person down the page; if the answer needs thought, it should not.

The rule

The bar holds the actions and the buttons stay themselves: the row decides the split, and no button in it gains a width, a radius or a colour it would not have anywhere else.

The anti-rule

Never use it as the bet control. A sticky bar at the foot of a phone that commits money is betpanel's dock, which the width swaps against a panel at 760 and which R3 counts at most one of; this bar is a way ONWARD from a screen, not a way to spend on it. They look identical at 360 and the difference is whether pressing costs anything.

Predicted: it has not happened. The two have never met on a screen, and R3 and R7 are why - a bet control does not stand on a list, and this bar's three screens are a wallet, a profile and a document. Recorded because the shapes are the same and the screens are one product decision apart.

What it arranges, and whose the states are

Sticky to the foot of the column, above the bottom nav, with the actions in source order and no reordering at any width. The .flat variant is the same bar where the page does not scroll, and choosing between them is the one decision this pattern leaves to a person: a column that scrolls under the bar keeps the stone, a page that ends where the bar is takes it away.

Nothing here has a state. What answers a pointer is the button inside it, photographed on that component's page, and the account surface behind it is declared static because a bar is not a control.

Where it stands

The existence proof. A composition is a pattern here because it stands on three or more screens, so this list is not a footnote: it is the reason the file exists. Counted from the markup of all 105 painted screens, not from a list.

Three, which is the threshold exactly. This is the thinnest proof in the folder and it is not hidden behind a phrase: one of these three losing its bar takes the pattern below the line that admitted it.

The file

A pattern loads LAST in components/index.css, after every component, because it has to be able to place what it holds. It carries no colour at all, and gate 23 fails the build if one appears.

components/patterns/action-bar.css
/* components/patterns/action-bar.css
   Pattern: the action bar. One or two actions held at the foot of a column,
   sticky by default and flat where the page has no scroll to hold them against.

   Assembled from: button.
   Stands on: 3 ui-visual screens (my-profile, wallet, how-it-works). Three is
     the threshold and this clears it exactly, which is worth saying out loud
     rather than rounding up: it is a pattern by one screen.
   Moved here from: components/account.css, five rules and half of a sixth, cut
     and not copied.
   THE HALF, AND THE EXCEPTION IT MAKES. The bar's own surface stays in
     account.css: a stone, a hairline along the top edge and two rounded top
     corners. That is NOT the page plate, so it does not follow .feed-inner into
     base.css, and it is not composition, so it does not come here. It is the
     paint of a component, and this is the one place in this step where the rule
     "composition out, plate to the frame" needed an exception rather than being
     forced. The same split runs through .flat: the position is here and the
     three declarations that take the surface away are there.
   Colour goes through a role, geometry straight from a primitive. No em dash. */

.cta-bar{display:flex}
.app-case .cta-bar{position:sticky;bottom:0;padding:var(--space-12);gap:var(--space-8);margin-top:var(--space-12);z-index:var(--z-dock)}
.app-case .cta-bar>a{flex:1}
.app-case .cta-bar>a>button{width:100%}
/* And the same sentence for a bare child, which the bar has as often as it has
   an anchor. It arrived here on 2026-08-03 out of the button rule account.css
   was carrying: how the children of a row share the row is the arrangement, and
   the arrangement is what this file is. Written as a CHILD selector, unlike the
   rule it came from, because a button nested inside the anchor above is not a
   flex item of this bar and flex:1 on it means nothing. */
.app-case .cta-bar>button{flex:1}
.app-case .cta-bar.static{position:static}
.app-case .cta-bar.flat{position:static;padding:var(--space-8) 0 0}
