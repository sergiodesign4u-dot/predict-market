Unmeasured, and what that word was protecting

This component stands on none of the five anchor screens the kit was rebuilt from, so ui-kit/docs/inventory.md filed it as outside the core, unmeasured and refused to give it a level. That was not laziness. The level formula answers "1" for a component built out of its own class names, and it answers "1" for a component nobody has read at all, so a level printed without a reading is a guess wearing a declaration's clothes.

Walked today, the reading is unambiguous: three bars on how-it-works, my-profile and wallet, and every one of them holds two .btn. It HOLDS button, so it is level 2, and the word came off.

The plate face is a stone, a hairline along the top edge, two rounded top corners and the padding that goes with them; .flat is three declarations that take all of it away and leave the row. So the thing this file draws stands once in the product, and the majority of its placements are the negation of it.

That is a real question rather than a defect: either the plate is the face and two screens are wrong, or the flat row is the face and the plate is the variant. Nothing in the repository says which. docs/backlog.md 63.

Eight rules left, and they left because they were the button family

Until 2026-08-03 this file drew the buttons: the quiet rest, the brass first child, both hovers and both presses. They are in button now, because they were the family: the same control ground, the same 1px hairline, the same primary ink and the same 10 corner that three other one-off button classes each declared separately, plus the one hover the majority already had right.

The markup here is a bare <button> with no class, so the class map could not see the divergence either. A person assembling an action bar was hitting a button rule the button page had never heard of, and there was no reading anywhere in the repository that would have shown it.

The arrangement went one step further, to components/patterns/action-bar.css, which already said how an anchor child shares the row. What stayed here is the exception the pattern cut declared out loud: a stone, a hairline and two corners are not composition, and they are not the page plate either, so they stay with the component that draws them.

The rule

A bar owns its surface. The controls are the family's and the arrangement is the pattern's. Three owners, one row, and each of the three can be read on its own page.

A level is a reading, not an arithmetic. This component was called unmeasured for a day rather than called level 1 forever.

The anti-rule

Never let a bar draw a button. Eight rules did, on a bare element with no class, which is the version of the defect no class map can find.

Never leave a component whose default face is worn once. Two of three placements are the negation of what this file draws, and nothing says which one is the component.

Never print a level for something nobody has rendered. The formula answers 1 for an atom and 1 for an unread file, and the two are indistinguishable in the output.
