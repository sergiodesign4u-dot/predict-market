Why the system looks like this

Five attribute pairs, decided at Concept. Each is "this, not that", each traces to a line of research rather than to a mood, and each turned into something you can point at in the system. The left column is the pair as concept/docs/concept.md states it; the middle is what it became here; the right is where you can go and see it.

the pair, from Conceptwhat it means in the systemwhere you see it
A1High-contrast and alive, not muddy and flatThe canvas is graphite `#0f1013` and not white, and there is exactly ONE accent on it: matte brass `#c7a24e`. Contrast does the work colour usually does, so the page can be quiet and still feel live. Every surface is a two-stone emboss (a lit lip on top, a cast shadow under) rather than a flat fill, which is where the aliveness comes from now that the glow and the dot-grid of the earlier direction are gone.
the graphite ramp and the brassthe two-stone plates and the bevels
A2Credible and controlled, not hyped or degenBrass appears on identity, on the active state and on ONE call to action per zone, and nowhere else. That is not restraint for its own sake: an accent used as decoration stops meaning "press this", and a money product that shouts reads as a casino. The rule is executable, not advisory, and R1 in the contract is the counted version of it.

A3A spectator's clarity, not a trader's terminalNumbers are always mono, because a proportional figure looks like prose and a mono one looks like a reading. The market state is a pool and a depth curve, never an order book. The odds bar carries the outcome colour so the YES / NO buttons can stay quiet tinted shapes instead of two loud fills.

The brand plate

Every value below is the shipped one, from DESIGN.md, resolved live by the page you are reading rather than printed as a swatch: if a token moved, this moved with it.

pagevar(--bg-page)graphite, and the reason nothing else has to shout
platevar(--bg-plate)the inset stone a screen stands on
cardvar(--bg-card)the floating stone a card is cut from
brassvar(--color-action)the one accent: identity, active state, one action per zone
YESvar(--outcome-yes)reserved. Never an accent, never a brand colour
NOvar(--outcome-no)the other half of the same reservation
inkvar(--text-primary)bone, warm, on a faintly cool stone

Three families and nothing else: Space Grotesk for display, DM Sans for body, IBM Plex Mono for every number. The mono is not decoration. A figure set in a proportional face reads as prose, and a person checking what a market says needs it to read as a reading.

One divergence, named rather than buried. The taste locked electric violet as the brand accent and acid lime as the highlight, from the MELEE reference. The product ships matte brass on graphite. That was a designer decision taken on the stand on 2026-07-16, not a drift: brass answers the same complaint the violet was chosen for (an accent with no contrast against its ground) while reading as weighty rather than neon, which serves A2 and the documented "this looks like crypto, so it looks like a scam" fear better than lime did. Anyone re-reading the taste table in concept/docs/concept.md should treat brass as final.

Where the values came from

The short answer is that none of them was chosen at this stage. The system was read out of a product that already worked, and every step of that reading is still on disk.

  1. A pixel of the shipped plate

    The system was not designed on a blank page. It was READ OUT of screens that already worked: every colour in it was sampled from the painted product rather than picked, which is why the graphite steps are 11.7 L* apart instead of a round number.

  2. The audit that wrote down what each value was doing

    Twenty four tables of hex, and beside every value the declarations it was read from. This is the step where a colour stopped being a number and became a ROLE: not "#1c1f24" but "the surface a chip sits on".

  3. The direction locked at Concept

    Vault: graphite, one matte brass, reserved green and red. The reference table above is where that came from, and it is the only part of the chain that is a judgement rather than a measurement.

  4. The theme, which is the proof the roles are real

    A light theme was built over the same roles, and NOT ONE PRIMITIVE was redefined. That is the test a rebrand cannot run: the ground inverts, the ink inverts, and the action still has to read as the action, which is only writable if the reason a colour is in a place is stored somewhere. It found twelve holes, and every one of them was a component reading a raw value.

  5. `components/tokens.css`, in two levels

    A primitive is a value; a role is why that value is in that place. A component may read a role and never a colour primitive, and gate 13 fails the build when one does.

And the contrast figures are measured, not computed. That belongs here rather than in a footnote, because it is part of where a value comes from. getComputedStyle() returns what the author wrote, and modern css lets an author write a colour that is not a literal: color-mix(in oklab, ...) comes back verbatim, and a regex reading it as sRGB bytes reported a pale brass banner as near black. Every ratio in this system is taken by handing the computed string to a 2D canvas, filling one pixel and reading it back, with the ancestor stack composited for alpha. A figure that did not come from that path does not go in the token file.

How to use it

Four questions, four doors. They are four and not three on purpose: the last one is the question nobody thinks to ask.

Which component do I take?Every component file, its page and the level it sits at, in the order the cascade loads them. Start here when you know what the thing is.overview.htmlIs the arrangement already built?Six compositions that repeated on three or more screens, each framed live. Start here when you know what the SCREEN is, before you know what is in it.patterns.htmlWhat are the rules?The contract: two token levels, what a component may read, where a change goes, and the reasoning behind each. Read once, then read the row you need.architecture.htmlWhat am I not allowed to do?Nine rules of use: how many of a thing a screen may carry and where it may not stand. This is a door of its own and not a paragraph of the one above it, because nobody looks for a prohibition until after they have broken it.architecture.html#rules-of-use

And when you are not sure which

  1. Look for a pattern first. If the screen you are building is doing the job an arrangement already exists for, take the whole arrangement, including the parts you think you do not need.
  2. No pattern: assemble from components. Two screens sharing a shape is a coincidence until a third one turns up. Building it out of components is the correct answer, not a fallback.
  3. No component either: that is an order for the SYSTEM, not an exception for the screen. A screen that styles itself is the one failure this whole structure exists to prevent, and it is the failure that looks most like getting the work done. A new component is a css file, an @import at the position the cascade computes, a stand page and a row in the inventory. It is five minutes of work and it is never the wrong call.

How to grow it

One sentence, and the full version is written at the next step: a new thing is built in the system first and used on a screen second, never the other way round. The order is the whole rule, because a thing built on a screen and lifted afterwards arrives carrying that screen's assumptions, and nobody ever finds them again. The mechanics are in how to add a component and how to add a pattern.
