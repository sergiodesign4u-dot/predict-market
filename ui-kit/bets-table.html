All three are read and none of them is touched. There is no control in this file: no row links anywhere, nothing hovers, nothing presses. That is the correct answer for a list whose job is to let you check a number, and it is why the whole file is 49 lines.

The outcome word in a holder row takes the outcome text role, YES green and NO the figure red, which is the same rule the odds bar's labels follow: text answers to 4.5:1 and a band answers to 3:1, so they are two roles rather than one colour.

One trap, and it is the second sighting of it

A column head inside one of these lists is nested under a section that carries its own label rule, and the label rule sets properties this one does not: uppercase, tracking and a brass ink. So a nested head has to answer for each of them rather than for the one that looked wrong.

It is the same shape and the same afternoon as the event detail screen. Specificity is per PROPERTY, and a rule that wins on one declaration has not taken the element.

The rule

A list you read carries no state. No hover, no press, no cursor: a row that answers a pointer is promising a destination it does not have.

A figure and a band do not share a role. Text clears 4.5:1 and a surface clears 3:1, so the same green is two tokens.

The anti-rule

Never show one of three lists and call it the component. Two of them are behind tabs and measure 0x0 until the radio changes.

Never assume a rule that wins has taken the element. It won on one property and lost the other three.

Never give a read-only row a pointer. The tap goes nowhere and the row has said it will.
