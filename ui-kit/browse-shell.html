Browse shell

A narrow rail beside a content column, stacked under 900px and side by side above it. It owns none of what it holds and paints none of it: what it says is where the two columns are, in which order they stack, and that the content column may not be pushed off the screen by its own contents.

Live

Cut out of event-feed-crypto-empty.html byte for byte, with the ancestor chain it has there, and painted by components/index.css alone. Every string is the shipped microcopy, because none of it was retyped. The rail is the point of this pattern and the base category pages do not show it: on those nine the markup ships <nav class="subcat" hidden> and a page script fills it. Twenty four screens carry it filled and visible, and every one of them is a STATE screen, so the scene is cut from one. Nothing here is set or unset by the generator: this is that screen's markup.

This is the only place the pattern exists outside the product. A component has a specimen as well as a stand; a pattern has neither, because step 3 took patterns out of gate 24's corpus deliberately: that gate compares what a component contains against what it contains on the screens, and a pattern contains whatever the screen puts in it. So this frame is not an illustration of the pattern, it is the test of it.

Anatomy

What each part is and whose it is. A part that belongs to a component links to that component's page, because the answer to "how does this look" is there and not here: this file decides placement and nothing else.

.cat-mainthis patternThe content column. flex:1;min-width:0, and the min-width is what stops a long word inside a card from pushing the rail off screen.

Everything inside, read from the markup: catnav (.subcat-head), chip (.chip), filters (.filter-menu), state-block (.state-block), button (.btn). The class in brackets is what put each component in the list, through the same ownership map that computes the level and orders the cascade. A name here that surprises you is worth following: a class declared in the wrong file makes a pattern read as holding a component it has never seen.

Variants

.cat-layout:not(:has(.subcat))

No rail, so nothing to align to: the column takes the full height instead of hanging at the top of a row it is the only member of. Specificity 0,3,0 against 0,1,0, so this is decided by the selector and not by the order of the files.

What it composes

The COMPONENTS in it, named by a person. The Anatomy above answers which class is which; this answers what each part contributes, which is the question a class list cannot reach.

  • catnav - the sub-category rail, on a category page. Its chips, its counts and its sticky offset are its own.
  • toc - the same slot on a document page, holding a table of contents instead. This is the thing Terms taught: the rail is a SLOT, and the pattern does not know which component is standing in it.
  • filters - the controls that stand in the content column's head on most of the 77.
  • state-block - what stands in the content column when there is nothing to show, on 38 of them.
  • button - the exit inside that block, because on an empty screen it is the only thing to press.

When to use it

When a screen browses a set - a category, a list of bets, a wallet ledger, a profile, a document - and there is a second, narrower axis that belongs BESIDE the content rather than above it.

Fourteen different sets of content stand in the column across the 77 screens, and that is the argument for a pattern rather than a component: the arrangement is stable and what it holds is not.

With the rail empty, freely. The variant :not(:has(.subcat)) gives the column the full height instead of hanging it at the top of a row it is the only member of, and it is decided by specificity (0,3,0 against 0,1,0) rather than by file order.

By hand: when the two columns are about ONE subject rather than a set and its axis. A screen where the side column acts on what the main column is showing - a bet panel beside an event, a preview beside a form - is detail-shell, and the difference is not the widths, it is what the side column is FOR. The test: if the side column's contents change when the main column's contents change, it is not this pattern.

The rule

The rail goes first in the markup and the column second, at every width: the stacked order under 900px is the source order, so a rail written after the column would land under it on a phone and beside it on a desktop.

The anti-rule

Do not put the page's own surface on it. The two-stone plate under this shell is in components/base.css with the rest of the frame, because it is what the PAGE is made of and not what this arrangement is, and a pattern that carries a background is a component with the label filed off.

Seen: gate 23 in ui-kit/_check_kit.py fails the build on a pattern that carries a colour, and it exists because the pattern extraction had to decide, file by file, which declarations were arrangement and which were paint. ui-kit/docs/backlog.md S11 is the same question answered the other way in a component: eight rules that were paint had been living in components/account.css beside one line that was arrangement, for three stages.

What it arranges, and whose the states are

Column at 360, row at 900 and up, with the gap changing with the direction, because the distance between two columns is not the distance between two stacked blocks. The content column takes flex:1;min-width:0, and the min-width is the load-bearing half: without it a long word inside a card pushes the rail off the screen.

The rail's own sticky offset is NOT here. top:120px belongs to catnav, and ui-kit/docs/backlog.md S12 records why that number cannot be a token and why the three copies of it were already meant to differ: toc sits at 66px in this same slot, because a document page has no category bar for the header strip to condense into.

No states of its own. Everything that answers a pointer in this shell belongs to catnav, filters, toc or button, and each is photographed on its own page.

The file

A pattern loads LAST in components/index.css, after every component, because it has to be able to place what it holds. It carries no colour at all, and gate 23 fails the build if one appears.

components/patterns/browse-shell.css
/* components/patterns/browse-shell.css
   Pattern: the browse shell. A sub-category rail beside a content column.

   Assembled from: catnav (the .subcat rail), feed, state-block, loadmore, tabs.
   It owns none of them and paints none of them; it says where they stand.
   Stands on: 76 ui-visual screens (event-feed x41, active-bets x9,
     notifications x5, public-profile x4, my-profile x3, wallet x3, favorites x3,
     event-detail x2, maintenance, how-it-works). Fourteen different sets of
     content across those 76, which is the argument for a pattern rather than a
     component: the arrangement is stable and what it holds is not.
   Moved here from: components/catnav.css, five rules, cut and not copied.
     .cat-layout and .cat-main were catnav's only because catnav was the first
     file that needed them; nothing in either rule is about a category.
   The SURFACE those two stand on did not come with them. The two-stone plate is
     the page's material, not this arrangement's, and it is in base.css with the
     rest of the frame.
   Colour goes through a role, geometry straight from a primitive. No em dash. */

.cat-layout{display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:var(--space-12)}
.cat-main{flex:1;min-width:0}
@media(min-width:900px){
  .cat-layout{flex-direction:row;align-items:flex-start}
}
/* The gap belongs to the wide arrangement, and it is written apart from the
   direction above because at 900px the rail arrives and the distance between
   two columns is not the distance between two stacked blocks. */
@media(min-width:900px){
  .cat-layout{gap:var(--space-20)}
}
/* WITH NO RAIL THERE IS NOTHING TO ALIGN TO, so the column takes the full height
   instead of hanging at the top of a row it is the only member of. 0,3,0 against
   the 0,1,0 above, so specificity decides this and not the order of the files.
   THE SELECTOR NAMES BOTH RAILS because the slot takes more than one: a page
   with a .toc and no .subcat is still a page with a rail. Widening it is a
   no-op on every screen that has no .toc, which is 76 of the 77, and that was
   measured rather than reasoned. */
.app-case .cat-layout:not(:has(.subcat,.toc)){align-items:stretch}

/* ---------------------------------------------------------- the reading column
   THE SECOND THING THE MAIN COLUMN CAN BE. Until now it held a grid or a list,
   and both of those bring their own distance: a grid has gap, a .pos-list has
   gap. A stack of PROSE blocks has neither, and the page that first needed one
   showed exactly what that costs. ui-visual/terms.html put fourteen blocks in
   .cat-main and every one of them touched the next, because the only vertical
   rhythm this chain declares is .feed-inner's gap in base.css, and .feed-inner
   had one child.

   THE CONTAINER OWNS THE DISTANCE, NOT THE BLOCK. That is the whole rule and the
   reason it is written here rather than as a margin on .feed-seo: a margin on a
   block is a fact about that block, so the next block dropped into the stack
   arrives with no distance again and the defect returns one screen later. The
   reset on the children is deliberate and is the same sentence from the other
   side: a block in this stack may not carry its own vertical margin.

   THE MEASURE IS THE OTHER HALF. --container-read is 800px and belongs to the
   feed's SEO plate; on 13px prose that is 89ch measured, against the 60 to 75
   DESIGN.md decided. A long document is where a reader sees the difference, so
   the column takes --container-doc and centres itself in whatever the rail
   leaves. */
.read-col{display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:var(--space-16);max-width:var(--container-doc);margin-inline:auto}
.read-col>*{margin-block:0}
/* A prose block is a stack too, one level down, and it is the same argument:
   .feed-seo p carries margin:0 because the block was designed as one paragraph
   under one heading. Fourteen sections of two to five paragraphs is the first
   time it held more. */
.read-col .feed-seo{display:flex;flex-direction:column;gap:var(--space-8)}
.read-col .feed-seo h2{margin-bottom:0}
