Product Voice & Microcopy

The contract · voice.md

Five principles

Ordered by how often they fire, most-used first. The context sets the tone: crypto money, on real-world events, among strangers, with no regulatory badge we may show — so trust matters more than wit.

1 · Explain the number, never just show it

Every number carries the one plain reason it is what it is; we never leave a bare figure to speak for itself.

Example
“YES 38%. Funding talks have stalled twice this quarter — that is what is holding the price down.”
Anti-example
“YES 38%” (and nothing else).  ·  “Buy Yes 37¢.”

Why: The confirmed differentiator, not a nicety. research.md: competitors have “no clear why this number explanation”; jtbd.md FJ2: the user wants to understand why the market prices it so, not bet blind.

2 · One plain sentence of trust, before the ask — never borrowed authority

Before we move a user's money we say what happens to it in one plain sentence, and show the cost before Confirm; we never dress up authority we do not hold.

Example
“Your USDC is held 1:1. We never lend it without your permission.”  ·  “You pay a 2% fee only if you win. Nothing on a loss.”
Anti-example
“Bank-grade security.”  ·  “Fully protected.”  ·  a regulatory badge we cannot show.

Why: benchmark-trust.md: regulatory badges require real licenses we do not hold; matching their authority signals with no history “reads as fake.”

3 · Speak to a spectator with an opinion, not a trader

We use the words a news-follower already owns — event, bet, YES / NO, “if YES wins you get $X” — and keep trading-desk jargon out of the primary path.

Example
“Bet YES — if it happens, $5 becomes $13.20.”  ·  Profile: “Your record: 12 calls, 9 right.”
Anti-example
“Buy 13 YES shares at $0.38 avg, spread 2¢.”  ·  “Provide liquidity to this market.”

Why: ux-patterns.md: “a table with prices in cents is the language of a trader, not a spectator.” personas.md: identity = analyst, action = bet.

4 · Design the loss; mark the win without lighting a fuse

After a result we explain what happened in plain words and offer one calm next step. We acknowledge being right without confetti, and never nudge “bet again” after a loss.

Example
Win: “You were right. +$13.20.”  ·  Loss: “Here's what happened: Congress passed a stopgap bill — no shutdown.” + one next step.
Anti-example
“Congratulations, you WON! Ride your streak — bet again now!”  ·  after a loss: “Don't give up — win it back!”

Why: personas.md: confetti without friction accelerates loss-chasing; platform betrayal is the documented #1 churn driver — more than losing a bet.

5 · Say the specific, provable thing — not a superlative

We name the concrete fact — a date, a source, a real count — instead of marketing scale; if we cannot prove it today, we do not claim it.

Example
“214 markets resolved since March 2026. Verify every one on-chain.”
Anti-example
“The world's most trusted prediction market.”  ·  “Join millions of winners!”

Why: research.md competitor language: everyone leads with a superlative (“World's Largest”). We can't match that at launch — our difference is the specific, checkable fact.

Rules by element

Microcopy

The principles applied at the level of a single element — each with one real line from our own screens.

01

Button

An action verb whose object shows the result; never a bare OK / Next / Submit or a naked “Bet”.

Ours: “Confirm bet” · “Add funds” · “Browse events”

02

Screen heading

Name the place in the words the user already uses; not a trading-desk label.

Ours: “My Bets” · “Wallet” · “How events resolve”

03

Form field

Label says what to enter, hint says how, error says exactly what to fix.

Ours: “Amount to add” + “Minimum $10 …” + “The minimum is $10. Enter $10 or more.”

04

Empty state

Say why it is empty and give the one next step; never a bare icon or “Nothing here yet”.

Ours: “No active bets yet. … put a real stake on it.” + “Browse events”

05

Error

Say what happened and what to do next; no apology, no joke, no filler.

Ours: “Couldn't load your wallet. Your funds are safe … Try again.”

06

Loading

Stay silent (a skeleton) or name exactly what is loading; never a hype line.

Ours: “Registering your bet on-chain …” (else a silent skeleton)

07

Success

Name the fact and the next step; mark being right, but do not celebrate.

Ours: “You were right. +$13.20. The market resolved YES, the side you held.”

08

Dangerous / irreversible

Before the press, say what will happen and what cannot be undone.

Ours: “On-chain transfers are final and cannot be reversed — check the address before you confirm.”

The rewrite

Every UI line on all 99 wireframe pages was rewritten line-by-line against voice.md (Steps 05–14), one screen family at a time, with a before / after row logged for each change. User-written content — event questions, comments, usernames — is never touched. The closing audit found 0 lexicon or forbidden violations left in product copy.

The panel that moved from trader to spectator

The Event Detail social panel (Comments / Holders / Positions / Activity) was built on trading-desk vocabulary. It was rewritten to a spectator reading (Principle 3):

Same action, same label

The closing reconciliation confirmed one action carries one label everywhere: retry is always “Try again”, funding is always “Add funds”, the feed CTA is always “Browse events” (the last stray “Back to Trending” unified), the commit is always “Confirm bet”, and auth is always “Sign in / Sign up”.
